# Teach whole-turn tactics through labels, not knobs

Games built like COLONY: ONE all teach the same lesson. Strength comes from an evaluator that **sees a whole turn's tactic statically**, trained on **labels from a search that also sees it**. It does not come from hand-tuned prior bonuses. Arimaa's champion bot Sharp got its largest search gain, **+80 Elo**, from generators that emit complete multi-step tactics, and every strong Arimaa program detects captures with pattern code rather than searching for them ([Wu 2015](https://icosahedral.net/downloads/djwu2015arimaa.pdf)). Stockfish 11 weights a *safe* check 5–7× an unsafe one ([SF11 evaluate.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/sf_11/src/evaluate.cpp)). Texel-style tuning only learns what its labels can see ([CPW Texel](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)). The team's own numbers agree. Removing any one of hand_v4's prior terms leaves the model **at parity with v4f (48–55%)**. Value features with a joint refit added **5 pp**. Teacher-labelled distill students ignore their base: v3hb scored **50% vs v3** ([ablate.log](../agents/hand/data/ablate.log), [distill RESULTS](../agents/distill/RESULTS.md)).

The situation changed at 14:52, when **az_res_v20 took the seat** with 76–77% against az_res_v6. distill_v4s8 then lost to it **62/147 = 42%**, and retraining v20 on the same 133k positions is flat: v30 scored 53% and v31 48% ([RECOMMEND.md](../gate/RECOMMEND.md), [az log](../agents/az/log.txt)). The gate needs **≥41/60 (68%)** before 15:50 and ≥44/60 after 16:00, so only a +15–20 pp edge can still go live. Ranked by expected gain per hour, the team should:

1. Feed the az loop more v20-level self-play and rerun the v20 recipe on all the data around 15:35. This is the only lever with a realistic chance of passing the gate.
2. Put the existing `warm.py` sharpening on whatever is uploaded next, never on its own.
3. Spend 20–30 minutes probing v20 for the cross-game blind spots: shots on defended pieces, Kernel strikes from recapturable squares, and pre-bare trades.
4. Build the tactics bundle on the lean base only if that probe finds a real hole.

Latency limits every stacking idea. v20 already uses about **2.3–2.6 ms of a ~3 ms per-call budget**, and the FastNet4 re-base measured **4.3 ms**.

## Arimaa engines detect the whole turn's tactic without searching it

Arimaa has four steps per turn, the closest relative of our two actions per turn. Its best engine shows where multi-action strength comes from. Sharp's evaluation and quiescence search detect "goal in up to 4 steps" and "piece capturable within the steps left" directly. That extends the effective search depth "by a full four steps" at a cost cheap enough for every leaf. Which tactical moves enter quiescence depends on **how many steps are left** in the turn. When two piece-threat penalties overlap, Sharp **keeps only the maximum** "to avoid overpenalizing". Its tactical move generators propose whole multi-step tactics (quiet steps plus the capture) as one unit. They capture about **97% of the evaluation difference between expert moves and passing while generating 3% of legal moves**. Wu tuned them to minimise eval regret against the expert move, not to maximise coverage, and deliberately skipped quiet positions ([Wu 2015](https://icosahedral.net/downloads/djwu2015arimaa.pdf)). Double-move chess engines take the same structural route as our referee. ChessV treats each leg as its own ply and flips the side to move only after the last one ([chessvariants.org](https://www.chessvariants.org/index/listcomments.php?id=38026)). In Duck chess, the second leg exists "to prevent the opponent's best move in the next ply" ([Talkchess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=936168)). So the referee's one-node-per-action tree is standard. Full-turn knowledge can live in only two places: the value at the one-action-left node and the prior on the first leg.

For COLONY: ONE this means the threat accounting at one action left must differ from the accounting at two left, and threats should be aggregated as one strike per turn, not summed. With one action left, our own 2-action threats are worth little and the opponent's full 2-action turn comes next. With two left, the reverse holds. The coordinator confirmed two code gaps against this. First, `own_hang2` is gated only by the one-action-left gate, so the fit cannot separate the two cases ([fast4.py:71](../agents/hand/fast4.py)). Second, the 2-action hang maps H2 are **summed over squares per type** ([fast4.py:252-254](../agents/hand/fast4.py)), where Sharp's max or a top-2 would match "one strike per turn". Both live in FastNet4, the base the az team just tried. az_res4f_v1 measured **4.30 ms per batch-8 call, against 2.3–2.6 ms for v20**, and the validator rejected it under load ([az log](../agents/az/log.txt)). The fixes therefore matter for the live seat only if that base gets under the latency budget.

The prior side of the Arimaa lesson has already been tested and found wanting. The v4 bundle's defensive prior terms (2-action Kernel danger, guard-aware Kernel attack, early evacuation, opening sharpening) were each ablated from v4f. Every ablated model scored **48–55%** against the full v4f, so none of them carries v4f's 55% over v3. The gain came from the refit value features ([ablate.log](../agents/hand/data/ablate.log), [hand RESULTS](../agents/hand/RESULTS.md)). Hand-setting prior tactics without a refit cost **17 points** (v4p 38%). That is why the offensive "strike-setup" prior term from the Sharp analogy is dropped. Sharp's regret criterion transferred cleanly instead, as a label weight: the distill trainer's `QGAP` flag weights the policy loss by the teacher's best-minus-second gap, and distill_v5s8 was trained with it ([train.py](../agents/distill/train.py)).

## Shots and sacrificial guards break chess's "defended means safe"

Chess engines that have quiescence search treat static hanging-piece terms as redundant. Bob Hyatt called them "definitely redundant" with quiesce, and Stockfish's `Hanging` term is small at S(69,36) ([Talkchess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=232166), [SF11](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/sf_11/src/evaluate.cpp)). That argument does not carry over. The referee's 100-simulation PUCT calls our evaluator on raw tactical leaves with no quiescence behind it, so the static exchange has to live in the features. Here COLONY: ONE breaks the chess template in a way two research notes found independently. A **Trace or Daemon shot captures without moving**, so defending the target deters nothing. Daemon shots include the diagonals ("any direction", [level-4.txt:104](../level-4.txt)). The hand features multiply every hang term by (1 − hit_own) whatever the attacker type, so a defended piece under a shooter counts as safe. Shots decide real games: **3 of hand_v2's 10 reviewed live wins were Trace-shot Kernel kills**, and loss 6032 turned on a shot that removed the last guard ([hand_v2 review](../reviews/hand_v2.md)). In SEE terms, a shot is an exchange with no recapture on the target square, so `see1 = max(V·shot, V·move − cheapest_attacker·defended, 0)` can be built from the existing per-type maps with elementwise min and max ([CPW SEE](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Static_Exchange_Evaluation)).

The Kernel's bodyguards repeat the pattern. Guard priority is **ascending piece value** (15/25/30/45/50), so a strike on a guarded Kernel kills the *cheapest* adjacent guard while the attacker stays on its origin square. The real exchange is therefore the cheapest guard's value minus the attacker's value when the attacker's origin is recaptured. That is Stockfish's split between safe and unsafe checks (780–1080 vs 148) carried over to this game ([SF11](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/sf_11/src/evaluate.cpp)). The ablation fits: v4f's constant guard-aware bonus (`ek_guard 1.5`) ignores origin safety, and **removing it scored 110/200 = 55%** against v4f, at parity or better ([ablate.log](../agents/hand/data/ablate.log)). The live example is 6032 a24: the prior put 0.98 on an Ice strike from a recapturable square that killed a 25-point Exploit and lost the 50-point Ice. Removing `ek_guard` alone is therefore not the fix; replacing it with an origin-safe value is.

The bare-Kernel rule is the third static exception, and it is more common than the notes assumed. When only the Kernels remain, the position resolves at the end of the turn. Within Chebyshev distance 2 **the team that just finished its turn loses**; otherwise the deeper ring wins, and ties go to Magenta ([rules.md:64-66](../data/colony-one-env/docs/rules.md)). The hand value fires `Kdepth_diff_bare` only once no other piece is left. Yet bare-Kernel endings account for **34–64 of every 200 hand-vs-hand arena games (17–32%)** ([ablate.log](../agents/hand/data/ablate.log)). A closed-form "result if I capture their last piece now" feature is a few elementwise ops on the Kernel planes, ring indices and the 5×5 Chebyshev kernel. Pommerman adds a caution about danger terms. Raising the winners' pessimism level from 0 to 3 lifted win rate from **36.9% to 77.8%**, but one more level mainly added ties ([Osogami & Takahashi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10870)). Removing v4's 2-action Kernel-danger prior scored 52%, which fits the "too pessimistic, then passive" curve. Danger belongs in fitted value features, not in prior bonuses. Mining the real game is not an option: the published "Colony: One" is a September 2026 hobby app whose site gives no rules, strategy or piece values ([colonyonegame](https://colonyonegame.wordpress.com/the-game/)).

## Outcome labels from blind self-play cannot teach the fix

The tuning literature explains why the 2-action strike weights collapsed. Texel tuning fitted about 400 parameters on 64,000 games. Österlund warns that the engine "cannot learn what it doesn't understand", and he gained **+39.4 Elo by re-including non-quiet positions** ([CPW Texel](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)). Our hand fits use 1.5–2.3k games for 100+ weights. The coordinator checked the regulariser: at l2 = 1e-4 it only binds for features that fire in under ~0.1% of rows, so it does not explain `t2_own` fitting to about 0. The likelier cause is the labels. Self-play by models that cannot see 2-action strikes never punishes walking into one, so the game outcome carries no signal for the feature. Ethereal found that "increased diversity and the conversion of position to a quiet state" matter more than "WDL result precision", and it labels positions by playing out the PV of a deep search ([Grant, Tuning.pdf](https://github.com/AndyGrant/Ethereal/blob/master/Tuning.pdf)). NNUE trainers blend search score and result through λ ([CPW NNUE](https://www.chessprogramming.org/NNUE)). For hand refits, the fix is to fit on positions whose labels come from a search that sees the tactic. Two sources exist already. The az games store root Q and come from players stronger than the hand models. The distill teacher scores about 19k positions plus 113k child samples by alpha-beta over the full opponent turn with a capture quiescence. Fit to λ·Q + (1−λ)·z, and compare models on the first-fit validation number, because `fit.py:104`/`fit4.py:25` refit on all rows and the second "val acc" is in-sample.

The distill results confirm the mechanism. A student trained on teacher labels reached the same strength whether its fixed base was stopgap or the much stronger hand_v3 (v3hb vs v3 **30/60 = 50%**; v4hb vs v4s8 **47%**) ([distill RESULTS](../agents/distill/RESULTS.md)). The teacher's search already supplies the tactics, so a better base adds little. The az line is different, because its labels are its own 100-simulation Q and game outcomes. There, new data is now the binding constraint. Retraining v20 for twice as many steps (v30) scored 53/100 against v20. A variant whose value loss dropped from 0.42 to 0.31 on the same data (v31; the log does not record its Q share) scored **58/120 = 48%** ([az log](../agents/az/log.txt), [games10.jsonl](../gate/games10.jsonl)). This matches Expert Iteration's finding that online data from the improving apprentice beats batch retraining ([Anthony et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439)). KataGo's efficiency tricks are real: playout-cap randomisation **1.37×**, forced playouts with target pruning **1.25×**, game-specific input features **1.55×** ([Wu 2019](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1902.10565)). But they pay back over many generations, and only about six az generations fit before 15:40.

## Conclusion

The cross-game evidence shifts where the team should expect strength to come from. In a per-action PUCT with FPU 0 and no quiescence, the evaluator is the only place full-turn knowledge can live. The team already owns this game's equivalent of Sharp's tactical generators: the turn-aware alpha-beta teacher, and the az loop's own search Q. Those label pipelines, not prior bonuses, are what moved every model that improved, and the ablations show the prior knobs have no remaining gain. The shot and guard exceptions are genuine correctness gaps. But they pay only when the labels that fit them can see shots, which is why the probe on v20 should come before any code.

For today, the likeliest good outcome is that az_res_v20 keeps the seat, with at most one more upload of a loop-trained model carrying the warmup wrapper. The tactics bundle and search-labelled refits are the right investment for any further work on this game. Their payoff is a base that knows what Arimaa bots hard-code, under a residual trained on labels that can see it.
